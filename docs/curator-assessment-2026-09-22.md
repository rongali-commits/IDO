## Initial assessment

The live portfolio has genuine breadth: independent products, functioning demonstrations, screenshots, narrated walkthroughs, clear authorship, and honest scope boundaries. Those are valuable foundations. Its visual presentation is competent, but its primary impression is a software catalogue rather than an independent designer's portfolio.

| Area | Observation | Design response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First impression | A familiar rounded black split hero competes with a small dashboard recording. | A distinctive typographic opening, original interactive line sculpture, and a clear design-and-development proposition. |
| Art direction | Monochrome, uniform sans-serif type, and repeated containers offer little memorable identity. | Warm paper, ink, restrained vermilion, editorial serif accents, fine rules, and intentional differences in scale. |
| Project selection | Repeated media-and-description rows emphasize implementation categories and tool tags. | Four curated projects with varied editorial pacing and descriptions of the visual and experiential decisions. |
| Case-study hierarchy | Product purchase buttons and long offer exclusions precede the visual evidence. | Lead with the project and live experience; retain exact offers and boundaries in a dedicated section later. |
| Design rationale | Technical architecture is explained more explicitly than typography, hierarchy, and interaction choices. | Add evidence-grounded design decisions for Seatloom, FrameProof, Margin & Matter, and SignalRoom. |
| Motion | Existing product loops show functionality, but the portfolio has no signature interaction. | Original parametric SVG artwork, a keyboard-adjustable control, restrained reveals, and one consistent motion preference. |
| Responsive layout | Existing breakpoints provide a useful foundation; hierarchy needs new composition at narrow widths. | Recompose the hero and work into a single column, maintain clear actions, and verify all routes at several widths. |
| About and contact | Strong independent authorship, but SaaS engineering dominates the wording. | Make the designer/developer identity explicit while keeping the genuine engineering background. |
| Writing | Personal essays are a useful sign of curiosity but create a long second showcase on the homepage. | A compact editorial notes section preserves the writing without distracting from the design portfolio. |
| Credibility | Independent projects and limitations are clearly described. | Preserve those descriptions; do not add invented clients, awards, acceptance badges, or outcomes. |

## Contra context

Contra Labs' current jobs page describes an application involving a portfolio link, video, and evaluation. It emphasizes demonstrated work and creative judgment. Its general help article describes portfolio strength, articulate feedback, and an assessment; some examples in that article refer specifically to brand design, so they should not be treated as a verified web-designer-specific rubric.

- https://contralabs.com/jobs
- https://help.contra.com/en/articles/14757116-applying-to-the-contra-labs-network
- https://help.contra.com/en/articles/10628390-how-we-feature-work-on-contra-s-homepage

Contra's separate homepage curation guidance supports strong covers, multiple detailed images or videos, clear role descriptions, and live work. These are useful presentation principles, not an acceptance guarantee.

## Verification plan

Review the exact local production build. Check every sitemap route, internal links, image assets, metadata, redirects, custom 404, representative page screenshots, narrow/mobile/tablet/desktop widths, keyboard navigation, the artwork control, motion preference, and the project-brief preview. Run the existing assistant and brief regression tests without sending messages or paid provider requests. Record findings and repairs in the final assessment before requesting publishing approval.
